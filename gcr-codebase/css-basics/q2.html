A City Built on Legacy

Agra sits on the banks of the Yamuna and has been a seat of power for centuries, from the Lodi dynasty through the height of Mughal rule. Long before it became a global tourist destination, it was a capital of architecture, poetry, and trade.

Monuments That Define It

The Taj Mahal is the obvious icon, but Agra Fort and Fatehpur Sikri tell just as much of the story — red sandstone giving way to white marble, military architecture giving way to mausoleum. Together they chart the arc of an empire.

Petha and Everything Else

Agra's food culture is built around Petha, a translucent candied sweet made from ash gourd, but the city's street food scene — bedai, jalebi, and Mughlai-influenced curries — is worth the trip on its own.

Planning a Visit

Sunrise at the Taj avoids both the heat and the crowds. Most visitors pair a day at the Taj and Agra Fort with a half-day trip to Fatehpur Sikri, about 40 kilometers west of the city.

Best Time to Go

October through March brings cooler, drier weather — ideal for walking the fort's ramparts or spending a full morning at the Taj without the summer heat working against you.
